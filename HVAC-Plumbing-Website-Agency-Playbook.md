# The Plumbing / HVAC Website + Retainer Agency — Complete Launch Playbook

**Model:** Low/no upfront build cost → recurring monthly retainer bundling hosting + maintenance + call tracking + reviews + SEO.
**Target economics:** ~$50–150 hard cost per client/mo → charge $1,000–$2,500/mo. The recurring revenue compounds as you add clients.

*All prices verified at 2026 rates. Sources linked at the bottom.*

## 1. The core strategic decision: All-in-one (GoHighLevel) vs. best-of-breed

This is the single most important choice you'll make, and it changes your entire cost structure.

### The "best-of-breed" approach (stitching tools together)
You buy the best tool in each category and connect them:
- WordPress + hosting (site) + a page builder
- CallRail (call tracking) — $45–135/mo **per client**
- BrightLocal or NiceJob (reviews) — $39–75/mo **per client**
- A CRM + email/SMS tool
- A chat widget + booking tool

**Problem:** most of these price *per client*. At 10 clients, call tracking alone is $450–1,350/mo, reviews another $390–750/mo. Your costs scale linearly with clients — exactly what you don't want.

### The all-in-one approach: GoHighLevel (GHL)
GoHighLevel is purpose-built for *this exact business*. One platform replaces:
**website/funnel builder · CRM · 2-way SMS & email · call tracking · missed-call text-back · review/reputation management · web-chat widget · calendars/booking · automation/workflows · AI chatbot.**

The pricing is the killer feature:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $97 | $970 | **3** | Fine to start / test |
| **Unlimited** | **$297** | $2,970 | **Unlimited** | White-label, API. **The agency sweet spot.** |
| **Agency Pro (SaaS)** | $497 | $4,970 | Unlimited | Resell GHL as your own software + rebill telecom *with markup* |

The math that makes the model work: at **$297/mo you can run unlimited clients on one flat platform fee.** Your software cost per client *falls* as you grow — the opposite of best-of-breed. ([GHL pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing))

### Recommendation for someone starting out
**Start on GoHighLevel.** Begin on **Starter ($97)** for your first 1–3 clients to prove the model, then move to **Unlimited ($297)** the moment you have a 4th client (it pays for itself immediately). Graduate to **SaaS Pro ($497)** later only if you want to resell the platform itself and rebill SMS/phone usage *with markup* as extra profit.

**The one thing GHL does NOT replace well:** true SEO research (keyword/backlink/local-rank data) and top-tier hosting page-speed. Pair GHL with one cheap SEO tool (below). If a client is obsessed with a fully custom, high-performance site, build that one on WordPress — but run everything else through GHL.

## 3. Per-client unit economics (and why it compounds)

The magic of the flat-fee platform: **your tooling cost per client drops as you add clients**, while revenue scales linearly.

Assume: GHL Unlimited $297 + BrightLocal $39 + SE Ranking $55 + design $25 = **~$416/mo total fixed tooling for your whole agency**, plus ~$10–25/client telecom.

| Clients | Monthly revenue @ $1,500 ea. | Total fixed tools | Tool cost **per client** | Gross before labor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | $1,500 | $416 + ~$20 telecom | ~$436 | ~$1,064 |
| **10** | $15,000 | $416 + ~$200 telecom | **~$62/client** | ~$14,384 |
| **25** | $37,500 | $416 + ~$500 telecom | **~$37/client** | ~$36,584 |

The constraint is never software — it's **delivery labor**. Early on you do the work. As you scale past ~8–10 clients, you reinvest margin into a VA, a content writer, and an SEO contractor so your time goes to sales and account management. That's the flywheel.

## 4. Step-by-step launch plan

### Phase 1 — Setup & Legal (Week 1)
1. Form an **LLC** (your state or a service like Northwest/ZenBusiness; ~$50–300 + state fee). Protects personal assets and looks legitimate.
2. Open a **business bank account** + get an EIN (free from the IRS).
3. Set up **Stripe** for automated recurring billing (or use GHL's built-in payments).
4. Draft your **client contract/MSA** (see §8) — have a lawyer review the first one, then reuse it.
5. Create a simple **privacy policy + terms** for client sites (Termly/iubenda generate these cheaply).

### Phase 2 — Build your stack & a reusable template (Week 1–2)
6. Sign up for **GoHighLevel** (Starter to begin). Set up your agency profile and white-label branding.
7. Build **ONE killer plumbing/HVAC website template** inside GHL you can clone for every client: home, services, service-area pages, about, reviews, contact/booking, plus sticky click-to-call.
8. Pre-build your **automations**: missed-call text-back, new-lead instant reply, review-request sequence, appointment reminders.
9. Add **BrightLocal** + one SEO research tool. Create your reporting template.
10. Build a **demo site** for a fictional "Acme Plumbing" to show prospects.

### Phase 3 — Get your first client (Week 2–4)
11. Pick a tight geographic + trade focus (e.g. "HVAC companies in [your metro]").
12. Build a prospect list (Google Maps, GBP, local directories) — note who has weak/slow/no website.
13. Run the **outreach process in §6** (audit + Loom video offer).
14. Close 1–2 founding clients at a **discounted "founding rate"** in exchange for a testimonial + case study.

### Phase 4 — Deliver (Week 4–6 per client)
15. Run the **onboarding intake** (§7) and collect assets.
16. Clone your template, customize, launch within 7–14 days.
17. Set up call tracking number, review automation, GBP optimization.
18. Send the first monthly report — *show leads/calls generated*, not vanity metrics.

### Phase 5 — Scale (ongoing)
19. Move to **GHL Unlimited ($297)** at client #4.
20. Productize delivery into a repeatable checklist (§7).
21. Hire/outsource: VA → content writer → SEO contractor, in that order.
22. Ask every happy client for **2 referrals** + a video testimonial. Referrals are the cheapest acquisition channel in the trades.
23. Consider **GHL SaaS Pro ($497)** to rebill telecom with markup and/or resell the platform.

## 6. Client acquisition process (repeatable)

Plumbers and HVAC owners are busy, skeptical, and referral-driven. Sell outcomes (more booked jobs), not features.

**The repeatable loop:**
1. **Target tightly** — one trade + one metro at a time. Build a list of 50–100 local shops.
2. **Find the pain** — note slow sites, no mobile, no online booking, few/old Google reviews, not ranking in the Map Pack.
3. **The Loom-audit outreach** — record a 3-minute personalized video: "Hey [name], I looked at [company]'s online presence — here are 3 things costing you calls this week." Send via email + GBP message + a follow-up call.
4. **The foot-in-the-door offer** — "I'll rebuild your site free / $0 down, you just cover the monthly. If it doesn't bring you more calls, cancel." Low risk = easy yes.
5. **Demo ROI** — show your demo site + a competitor who's winning the Map Pack. Frame it as "your competitor is taking the calls that should be yours."
6. **Close on the retainer** — present 3 tiers, recommend Growth.
7. **Referral engine** — after the first month of results, ask: "Who are 2 other owners (not direct competitors) who'd want this?" The trades talk to each other.

**Other channels:** local BNI/chamber groups, trade association events, Facebook contractor groups, and partnering with suppliers (HVAC distributors, plumbing supply houses) who know every shop in town.

**Pitfalls:** don't pitch "websites" (commodity); don't target outside a niche (you lose the case-study compounding); don't skip the contract; don't guarantee #1 rankings.

## 7. Service delivery & monthly SEO checklist

### Onboarding intake — collect from every client
- Full service list + every city/zip they serve (→ becomes your page map)
- License #s, insurance, certifications (NATE, EPA, manufacturer)
- Logo (vector), brand colors; real photos (team, trucks, before/after) — arrange photography if missing
- Google Business Profile access (critical) + domain/DNS login
- Phone number for tracking, where leads should route (email/CRM/field software)
- Whether they use **Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan** ([pricing](https://www.rivetops.io/servicetitan-vs-jobber-vs-housecall-pro)) — your booking/lead handoff plugs into theirs
- Financing offered? Hours / emergency availability? Existing reviews?

### Build workflow (template-driven for speed)
Clone template → swap branding/content → generate service + service-area pages → add LocalBusiness/Service **schema markup** → set up tracking number + review + missed-call automations → QA mobile/speed → launch.

### Recurring monthly deliverables (the retainer)
- ✅ **Google Business Profile:** weekly posts, photo uploads, Q&A, category/service updates
- ✅ **Reviews:** fire automated review requests after each job; respond to all reviews; aim for steady review velocity
- ✅ **Local citations / NAP:** build new citations, fix inconsistencies (BrightLocal)
- ✅ **Content:** 1–4 new service/city pages or blog posts (per tier)
- ✅ **Technical:** monitor site speed, uptime, security updates, broken links
- ✅ **Rank tracking:** local keyword + Map Pack positions
- ✅ **Reporting:** calls generated, form leads, ranking movement, reviews gained — emailed + a quick call on higher tiers

**Pitfalls:** don't let sites go stale (a "set and forget" site ranks for nothing — that's churn); always keep NAP consistent; never neglect the GBP (it drives more local leads than the website itself).

## 8. Legal & contract essentials

Put these in **every** client agreement:
- **Scope of work** — exactly what's included per tier (and what's not, to stop scope creep)
- **Monthly fee + term** — e.g. 12-month initial term, then month-to-month; **auto-renew** clause
- **Payment terms** — auto-billed via Stripe/GHL on the 1st; late/decline policy; what happens on non-payment (pause service)
- **Ownership** — clarify who owns the **domain** (always the client) and the **website** (often *you* retain it while they're on retainer, especially with $0 upfront — this is your leverage and is standard)
- **Cancellation** — notice period; what transfers on exit
- **Performance disclaimer** — **never guarantee #1 rankings or specific lead numbers.** State that SEO results vary and depend on factors outside your control. This is the #1 way agencies get sued.
- **ADA / WCAG accessibility** — service-business sites are frequent ADA-lawsuit targets. Build to WCAG 2.1 AA (alt text, contrast, keyboard nav) and add an accessibility statement. Consider an accessibility widget. This protects both you and the client.
- **Privacy policy + terms** on every site (Termly/iubenda).

**Getting paid:** automate everything. Stripe or GHL recurring billing on a fixed date, card on file, auto-retry on decline. Manual invoicing is how agencies bleed cash. On GHL's $497 SaaS plan you can also **rebill the client's SMS/phone usage with a markup** as additional margin.

## 9. Top pitfalls to avoid

1. **Selling websites instead of leads.** A website is a commodity; booked jobs are not. Price and pitch the outcome.
2. **Per-client tool sprawl.** Best-of-breed stacks make costs scale with clients. Default to GHL's flat fee; add à-la-carte tools only when a client truly needs it.
3. **One-off builds with no retainer.** That's a job, not a business. The recurring revenue is the entire point.
4. **No contract / no auto-billing.** Both guarantee future pain.
5. **Guaranteeing rankings.** Legal and reputational landmine.
6. **Letting sites go stale.** No ongoing SEO/GBP work = no results = churn.
7. **Going too broad.** Niche down to one trade + one metro first; the case studies and referrals compound.
8. **Ignoring the Google Business Profile.** For the trades, the Map Pack often out-converts the website itself.
9. **Skipping ADA compliance.** Real lawsuit risk for service-business sites.
10. **Scaling delivery on your own back.** Productize and outsource before you burn out around client #8–10.
